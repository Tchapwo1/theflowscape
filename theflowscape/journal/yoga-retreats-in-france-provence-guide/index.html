Yoga retreats in France: a guide to Provence

Published 23 November 2026 · 8 min read

France is one of the easiest places in Europe to slow down. Fast trains, short flights and a deep culture of eating well mean you can leave a city on a Thursday and be practising yoga on a sun-warmed terrace the same evening. Of all its regions, Provence is the one most people picture first: lavender, stone villages, long lunches in the shade. This guide covers what a yoga retreat in Provence is actually like, when to go, how to get there, and how to choose a retreat that suits you.

Why Provence works so well for a yoga retreat

A good retreat is partly about practice and partly about place. Provence gives you both. The light is soft and golden for much of the year, the pace of rural life is unhurried, and the landscape — olive groves, vineyards, wild thyme and rosemary on the hillsides — invites you outdoors between sessions. Meals are built around local markets, olive oil, figs, cheeses and seasonal vegetables, which suits the simple, nourishing food most retreats aim for.

The Luberon Valley in particular has become a favourite for small retreats. It sits between Avignon and Aix-en-Provence, dotted with perched hilltop villages and ochre-coloured hamlets, and it is quiet enough that the loudest thing in the afternoon is often the cicadas. That quiet matters: the point of a retreat is to put down the noise of daily life long enough to notice how you actually feel.

What a weekend yoga retreat in Provence looks like

Most yoga retreats in France run either for a long weekend or for a full week. A three-day, two-night format is a good fit if you are coming from the UK or elsewhere in Europe: long enough to properly switch off, short enough not to need much holiday. Our own Provence Yoga Escape follows that weekend shape, and its sample itinerary gives a feel for the rhythm:

  • Day one — arrive and exhale. Check in to the bastide in the afternoon, then a gentle, restorative terrace session at golden hour and a shared welcome dinner.
  • Day two — move, scent and savour. Breathwork and meditation in the olive grove, an energising morning vinyasa, a long market-fresh brunch, a walking meditation through the lavender and herb-scented hills, a sunset sound bath with crystal singing bowls by the pool, and dinner under the stars.
  • Day three — radiate and return. A heart-opening morning flow, a closing circle, a long farewell lunch outdoors, and transfers back to the station.

Notice what is not in that list: no 6am bootcamps, no packed schedule. The best retreats leave generous empty space — time to swim, read in the courtyard, nap, or simply sit with a cup of tea. If a retreat timetable looks like a conference agenda, it probably will not feel restful.

Styles of practice to expect

Retreats in Provence tend to balance an active morning with a slower evening. In our approach, mornings bring solar practices such as sun salutations and vinyasa to build warmth and energy, while late afternoons and evenings shift to yin, restorative postures and sound. This “dual rhythm” means the weekend works whether you practise every day or have never been to a class. Good teachers offer modifications for every posture, so beginners and experienced practitioners can share the same mat space comfortably.

Sound baths have become a common feature of retreats in France. Lying still while bowls, gongs or chimes are played is deeply relaxing, and outdoors — under olive trees, with the hum of insects and a warm breeze — it is a very different experience from a studio session. If you are curious about sound work but unsure about it, a retreat is a low-pressure way to try it.

When to go: Provence through the seasons

Provence is at its best from late spring through autumn, and each season has a distinct character:

  • Late spring brings wildflowers, green hills and comfortable warmth — ideal for walking meditations and outdoor practice without the summer heat.
  • Summer is lavender season. Lavender typically blooms across the Luberon and the Valensole plateau from late June through July. Days are hot, so practice tends to happen in the cooler morning and evening hours, with long pauses by the pool in between.
  • Autumn means the grape and olive harvests, golden light and quieter villages once the summer visitors have left.

If seeing the lavender in bloom is high on your list, aim for the window from late June to July and check retreat dates carefully. If you prefer milder temperatures and fewer crowds, spring and autumn are often the more relaxing choice.

Getting there from the UK and Europe

One of the strongest arguments for a yoga retreat in France is how easy Provence is to reach without a long-haul flight:

  • By train: the high-speed TGV reaches Avignon in about 2 hours 40 minutes from Paris. From London, the Eurostar to Paris makes the whole journey possible by rail.
  • By air: Marseille Provence Airport (MRS) is well served from across Europe.

Our Provence bastide is about 45 minutes from Avignon TGV and under an hour from Marseille airport, and the retreat includes scheduled group transfers from Avignon TGV, so you do not need to hire a car. When comparing retreats, check whether transfers are included — rural Provence has limited public transport, and taxis from the station can add up.

Where you stay: the bastide

A bastide is a traditional Provençal country house, typically built of local limestone with terracotta tiles, thick walls that stay cool in summer, and courtyards shaded by plane or fig trees. Staying in one is a large part of the experience. The Flowscape’s Provence retreat takes place in an 18th-century stone bastide with cool limestone rooms, a secluded courtyard pool and views across the valley, with a choice between shared twin suites and a private master chamber. You can see the current options on the Provence retreat page.

Food on a Provence retreat

Food is one of the great pleasures of a yoga retreat in the south of France. Expect vegetarian Mediterranean cooking built around what is in season locally: market vegetables, figs, goat cheeses, crusty bread and good cold-pressed olive oil. On our retreats all meals are freshly prepared vegetarian dishes using local organic produce, and vegan, gluten-free and specific allergy needs are accommodated with notice. Whichever retreat you choose, ask how dietary requirements are handled before you book — a good host will be specific.

How to choose a yoga retreat in France

There are many yoga retreats in France, from large yoga holiday centres to intimate weekends in private houses. A few questions help narrow the field:

  1. How big is the group? Smaller groups mean more individual attention, a calmer atmosphere and a more personal experience. Across The Flowscape collection, groups are kept intentionally small, at 12 to 16 guests.
  2. What is the balance of practice and free time? Read the sample schedule. You want structure, but also space to rest.
  3. Is it suitable for your level? Look for clear language about modifications and beginners. No previous yoga experience is needed for our retreats.
  4. What is included? Check accommodation, meals, sessions, pool or spa access and transfers, so you can compare prices fairly.
  5. How easy is it to get to? A retreat 45 minutes from a TGV station is a very different trip from one that needs a long drive on mountain roads.
  6. Does the place itself matter to you? Some retreats could be anywhere. The best ones are shaped by their landscape — the walks, the food, the light.

What to pack

  • Light, breathable layers — mornings and evenings can be cool even when afternoons are hot.
  • Swimwear, sunglasses, a hat and high-factor sun cream.
  • Comfortable walking shoes for hillside walks.
  • A refillable water bottle.
  • A journal, a book, and as little else as possible.
