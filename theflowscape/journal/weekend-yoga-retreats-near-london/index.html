WEEKEND RETREATS

Weekend yoga retreats near London: what to expect and how to choose

The Flowscape Journal · 16 November 2026 · 7 minute read

A full week away is not always possible. A weekend yoga retreat in or near London gives you most of what people go on retreat for: unhurried practice, good food someone else cooks, time outdoors and a real break from screens. All of it fits between Friday afternoon and Sunday lunch, with no flights involved.

This guide covers how a weekend retreat is usually structured, the difference between staying over and joining as a day guest, the questions worth asking before you book, and how to arrive ready to switch off. Where it helps, we use our own London Yoga Escape in Richmond and Hampstead as a worked example.

Why choose a weekend retreat close to home?

The main reason is time. A long-haul or even short-haul retreat costs travel days on both ends, and many people come home tired from the journey. A London weekend lets you leave work on Friday and be on a mat that evening.

The second reason is that it is easier to keep the habit going. If the retreat is a short journey from home, the parks you walk in and the practices you learn are close by, and you can bring them into ordinary weeks. A weekend near London is also a gentle way to find out whether retreats suit you before you commit to a longer stay abroad.

The trade-off is that you are still near home, so it takes some effort to switch off. The best weekend retreats deal with this through their setting (quiet, green, away from the main roads) and through a clear rhythm that gives you nothing to organise.

What a typical yoga retreat weekend looks like

Most weekend retreats follow a three-part arc: arrive and slow down, a full day of practice, then a gentle close. Here is how that looks on our London weekend, as an example of the pace you can expect:

Friday evening: arrive and decompress

Check in late afternoon, have a welcome drink, then a slow candlelit yin practice to let go of the working week, followed by dinner. The aim of the first evening is to come down, not to work hard.

Saturday: energise, walk and restore

Saturday is the fullest day. On our programme it begins with a morning vinyasa flow in a glasshouse studio, then brunch, a walking meditation in Richmond Park or on Hampstead Heath, a twilight gong and sound bath, and a long-table dinner. Movement in the morning, time outside after lunch and deep rest in the evening is a good balance to look for in any programme.

Sunday: clarify and return

A heart-opening integration flow, a closing tea ceremony with intention setting, a celebratory lunch, and an unhurried departure in the afternoon. A good closing day helps you take something home rather than simply ending.

Tip: look at the balance between active and restful sessions in any itinerary. If every slot is vigorous yoga, you may come home more tired than you left. If there is no movement at all, it is more of a spa break than a yoga retreat.

Day pass or residential: which is right for you?

Retreats near London often offer two ways to take part.

  • Residential: you stay for the nights of the retreat. This is the more immersive choice: no evening commute, no morning alarm for the Tube, and the evening sessions flow straight into sleep. Our residential option is a two-night stay in a restored Victorian townhouse with a private walled courtyard.
  • Day pass: you take part in the daytime and evening programme but sleep in your own bed. This works well if you live nearby, have commitments at home, or simply sleep better in your own room. On our London weekend the day pass includes all yoga and sound sessions, meals and the guided park walks.

If you can, choose residential for your first retreat. Most of the value of a retreat comes from not switching context, and the journey home each night breaks that. If a day pass is what fits your life, it is still far more restorative than a series of drop-in classes.

Questions to ask before you book

A website can only tell you so much. These questions will tell you most of what you need to know about any weekend yoga retreat:

  1. How many guests are there? Small groups get more personal attention and feel calmer. The Flowscape keeps its sanctuaries to intimate groups of 12 to 16 guests.
  2. What level is it designed for? Look for sessions that offer gentle and stronger options so beginners and experienced practitioners can share the room. Our London sessions welcome every level.
  3. What exactly is included? Check meals, drinks, materials and any one-to-one sessions, so there are no surprises at the end.
  4. How are dietary needs handled? Ask early. Our London menu is plant-forward and seasonal, and gluten-free, vegan and allergy requests are catered for.
  5. How easy is it to get there? For a weekend, a short and simple journey matters. Our Richmond and Hampstead sanctuaries are about 25 minutes from central London by Tube and Overground.
  6. What is the cancellation policy? Read it before you pay. Ours is set out in our terms and cancellation policy.

In London or outside London?

"Near London" covers two quite different experiences. Retreats in the Home Counties and further afield offer countryside and distance, at the cost of a longer journey and often a car. Retreats within London's green edges, such as Richmond with its deer park and the Thames, or the wild ridges of Hampstead Heath, give you ancient trees and open sky within reach of public transport.

Neither is better. If you want to feel properly away, travel further. If the barrier for you is time and logistics, a retreat in the leafy edges of the city removes it.

How to prepare for your weekend

  • Clear your Friday afternoon. Arriving rushed is the most common way to lose the first evening.
  • Pack layers. Walking meditations happen outdoors whatever the British weather, so bring a warm layer, a waterproof and comfortable shoes, plus soft clothes for yoga.
  • Plan your digital pause. Set an out-of-office and tell people you will check messages once a day at most. Retreats such as ours build in an intentional break from screens.
  • Bring a notebook. Journalling sessions and closing intention rituals are more useful when you can look back at them later.
  • Keep Sunday evening light. Give yourself a quiet evening after you get home so the calm lasts into Monday.

When is the best time of year?

Every season has something to offer in London. Spring brings magnolias and fresh green parks, summer suits garden terraces and long evenings, autumn is ideal for sound baths and warm teas, and winter is made for candlelight and deep rest.
